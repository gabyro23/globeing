OPTION 1A — WRITTEN BRIEF

Globing — UX audit

gabyro23.github.io/globing — top issues, based on best-practice review

Globing lets people filter a world map and drag countries into a panel to compare population and land area. The core idea is sound. The issues below are about whether first-time visitors can discover and trust that interaction — ordered roughly by how many people they block.

01

Nav is English, content is Spanish

Why it matters: "About us / Contact / Download" sits above Spanish instructions and a Spanish loading message. Mixed languages on one screen read as unfinished or auto-translated, and it's not clear which audience the site is actually for.

Fix: Pick one language for the whole shell (nav, headings, buttons, empty/loading states). If both audiences matter, add a real language toggle rather than mixing per-element.

02

Drag-and-drop is the only way to add a country

Why it matters: Dragging a pin into a panel is a nice flourish but a poor primary interaction: it's imprecise on touch screens, invisible to keyboard/screen-reader users, and nothing on the map signals that countries are draggable in the first place.

Fix: Make click/tap-to-add the primary path (e.g. a "+ Compare" button on hover/tap), keep drag as an optional shortcut. Add a subtle hint the first time (e.g. a pulsing pin or "drag or click to compare").

03

No visible search or filter control

Why it matters: The intro text promises you can "filter countries," but there's no search box or filter chips in the initial view — someone looking for a specific country (e.g. Peru) has to hunt for it on a world map instead.

Fix: Put a persistent search/autocomplete field above or beside the map, always visible, not buried behind an icon.

04

Comparison panel has no example state

Why it matters: A first-time visitor sees an empty panel and instructions telling them to drag something into it — but never sees what a filled-in comparison actually looks like, so it's hard to know if it's worth the effort.

Fix: Pre-load two default countries into the panel on first visit, so the value is visible immediately, then let people swap them out.

05

Loading state is a bare line of text

Why it matters: "Cargando datos y mapa…" with no spinner, skeleton, or progress cue reads as a stall on slower connections — visitors can't tell if the site is working or stuck.

Fix: Show a map-shaped skeleton or spinner during load, and keep the message on screen only as long as it's true.

06

"Download" in the nav is ambiguous

Why it matters: It's not clear if this downloads a mobile app, the raw dataset, or the current comparison as an image/CSV. Ambiguous CTAs get ignored.

Fix: Rename to say exactly what it does — "Export comparison," "Get the dataset," or "Download app."

07

Instructions compete with the interface for attention

Why it matters: The hero is a paragraph of instructions rather than a clear, glanceable structure — there's no visual separation between "read this" and "use this," so the eye doesn't know where to land first.

Fix: Shorten the instructions to a short line or two, and let the interface itself (search field, map, panel) do the explaining through labels and affordances.

Note: this review is based on the site's markup and copy; a live click-through pass would surface additional issues (map performance, error states, exact drag behavior).
